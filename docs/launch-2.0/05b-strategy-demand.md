## 0. Method & honest constraints

**Method.** Fan-out WebSearch across English + Portuguese (for the "Brazilian" angle), then attempted primary-source fetches. Prioritised *recurring, multi-source* signals over single mentions.

**Network constraint (material — affects confidence).** As warned in the brief and consistent with Streams 02/05, large parts of the primary media-buyer web are **not directly fetchable** in this environment:

**Confidence labels used throughout:**
- **[data-backed]** — corroborated by ≥2 independent source types, or by a platform/vendor primary doc.
- **[directional]** — appears in multiple search extracts but mostly vendor/SEO content marketing, or single-source; believable but not independently proven.
- **[not-found]** — searched specifically and did **not** surface a reliable public source.

**Anti-fabrication note.** The named numeric strategies (151/135/115) and "Bruno/Brazilian method" were searched directly and repeatedly. Where the public web does not clearly define them, this report says **[not-found]** rather than guessing. That is itself a finding (it argues for FabAds authoring the canon).

### Reading the ranking
- **The center of gravity in 2026 is consolidation + automation** (rows 1–3): fewer ad sets, broad targeting, creative variety at the ad level, CBO + Advantage+. Any predefined strategy FabAds ships should default *toward* this, not toward 2018-era fragmentation.
- **The clearest "named, brandable, adopted" precedent is Meta's own "Power 5"** (row 3) — proof that a labelled bundle can achieve real currency. Numeric codes (151/135/115) do **not** have comparable public currency (see §3).
- **FabAds' structural wedge re-confirmed:** row 5 ("duplicate winners into new ad accounts") and row 6 (warm-up budget-stepping) are exactly the (account, page)-pair distribution + codified-warm-up jobs that `02-competitive.md` found *empty across every competitor*. Demand for these patterns is high; productized tooling for them is absent.

### Terminology map (how the category names "presets")
- **"Strategy"** → Revealbot/Bïrch, but means **automation-rule bundle**, not launch structure. (Naming collision risk: FabAds' "Launch strategy" ≠ Bïrch's "Strategy.")
- **"Tactic"** → Madgicx, vendor-authored **automation** presets (Revive/Stop Loss/Sunsetting/Surf).
- **"Template" / "preset"** → bulk launchers (AdsUploader/Adnova/AdManage) = **user-saved config/copy defaults**; Adnova/Smartly = **creative templates**.
- **"Blueprint" / "playbook"** → used in the *education* layer (Madgicx's recommended TOFU/MOFU/BOFU "blueprint"; agency "playbook" PDFs/decision-trees) — **content, not a software feature that builds the campaign.** [data-backed] ([Media Jet playbook](https://www.mediajetmarketing.com/blog/media-jet-paid-facebook-ads-playbook---2025-2026); [ad-times — 2026 Meta blueprint](https://ad-times.com/2026-meta-ads-blueprint/); [seomaven — agency playbook 2026](https://seomaven.pro/meta-ads-agency-playbook-2026/))
- **"Recipe"** → **[not-found]** in this space (it's an IFTTT/Zapier idiom; no FB-ads launcher surfaced using it).

### The whitespace (decision-useful)
The market has **automation presets** (post-launch, vendor-authored, one-click — Revealbot/Madgicx) and **config/creative presets** (user-authored — bulk launchers/Smartly). **Nobody occupies the middle: vendor-authored, named, *launch-structure* strategies that build the campaign→adset→ad skeleton from minimal input.** That middle is precisely what FabAds "Launch Strategies" proposes — and it is **empty**. The closest conceptual proofs that the *packaging* works are (a) Bïrch's one-click + shareable "Strategies" model and (b) Madgicx's named "Tactics" — both validate that **users adopt named, one-click, vendor-defined presets**; FabAds would be the first to apply that pattern to *launch structure*.

## 3. Publicly-documented definitions of 151 / 135 / 115 / Bruno-Brazilian (heavily caveated)

### 3a. Numeric codes "1-5-1" / "1-3-5" / "1-1-5" / "151" / "135" / "115"
**Finding: [not-found] as standardized public definitions.** Direct searches for these as Facebook-ad campaign-structure notation **did not surface any authoritative, consistently-defined public source.** Search tools repeatedly returned variants of *"there is no widely accepted public definition"* / *"these may be agency-specific or niche terminology not widely documented online."*

- **Most likely intended reading (inference, [directional], NOT confirmed):** the notation almost certainly encodes **campaigns–adsets–ads** counts (e.g. "1-5-1" = 1 campaign / 5 ad sets / 1 ad each). This is consistent with how the broader literature describes the **"3–5 ad sets per campaign"** sweet spot (§1 row 1) and the single-ad-set/"1-1-1" consolidation extreme (§1 row 7). But **no source explicitly labels a structure "1-5-1."** Treat the digit→level mapping as a reasonable hypothesis for the team to confirm, not a fact.
- **Caution flag:** the public consolidation consensus (§1 row 1) cautions that **>5 ad sets fragments signal** — so if any FabAds numeric strategy implies many ad sets, the team should be ready to explain why (e.g. it's a *testing* flow, where higher ad-set counts are accepted — §1 rows 8–9).

### 3b. "Bruno method" / "Brazilian method"
**Finding: [not-found] as a named, documented method.** Searches in both English and Portuguese ("método brasileiro", "estrutura brasileira", "Bruno") **did not surface any source that defines a method by either name.** No person named "Bruno" is publicly attached to a documented FB-ads structure in the results.

- **Closest real-world corroboration ([directional]):** Portuguese/Brazilian dropshipping content does teach a distinctive **high-ad-set rapid-testing structure** — **"1 campanha, 10 ad sets, 3–5 ads cada,"** validate in **48–72h**, kill below breakeven, scale winners to R$5–10k/day ([dropshippingnagringa — "100 mil/dia"](https://www.dropshippingnagringa.com/100-mil-dia-estrategia-de-teste-de-anuncios-no-facebook-ads/)). BR scaling content also leans hard on **broad targeting + the 20–30%/2–3-day budget rule** ([grecos.com.br](https://grecos.com.br/como-escalar-facebook-ads/)). So a **"Brazilian-style" = aggressive, high-ad-set, fast-cull testing** reading is *plausible and has some public footprint* — but **the label "Brazilian/Bruno method" is not what the sources call it.** If FabAds means this high-ad-set test structure, public usage loosely supports the *shape*; if it means something else, FabAds' definition simply governs.
- **Why the gap is expected, not a research failure:** these terms circulate in **Reddit, FB groups, Discords, and paid courses** — exactly the surfaces this environment **cannot fetch (403/blocklist)**. Absence of a clean public definition is consistent with the constraint and with these being **community/insider shorthand**. This *strengthens* the case for FabAds authoring the canonical definitions rather than importing a contested one.

## 4. Evidence-based implications for prioritization (final call deferred to the team)

Framed as **evidence**, not a decision. The team owns the definitions and the priority order.

1. **The demand evidence favors *consolidation/automation* defaults over fragmentation.** If FabAds is choosing which predefined strategy to build *first*, the patterns with the deepest, most-corroborated demand are **consolidation (few ad sets, broad, Advantage+, creative at ad level)** and the **ABO-test → CBO-scale** pipeline (§1 rows 1–2, [data-backed]). These also map cleanly onto the MOM's "**creative-testing flows vs scaling flows**" split. *Evidence-based read: a "testing" strategy and a "scaling" strategy are the two safest first bets by demand.*

2. **A named, one-click, vendor-defined preset model is validated — just not yet for launch structure.** Revealbot/Bïrch "Strategies" (one-click, shareable) and Madgicx "Tactics" (named, preset-value defaults) prove **users adopt named presets** (§2, [data-backed]). FabAds applying that proven *packaging* to **launch structure** is genuinely novel — the middle of the market is empty.

3. **FabAds' structural wedge is also a *demand* match, not just a competitive gap.** "Duplicate winners into new ad accounts" (§1 row 5) and "+20–30% every 2–3 days" warm-up (§1 row 6) are **high-demand patterns with zero productized tooling** (corroborates `02-competitive.md §8` empty band + `05-market-user.md` P0-3/JTBD-2). A predefined **scaling/redeploy strategy** that distributes across (account, page) pairs is where demand and whitespace overlap most sharply.

4. **Borrow Meta's "named bundle" precedent; avoid the naming collision.** "Power 5" shows a labelled strategy can get real adoption (§1 row 3). But **"Strategy" is already Revealbot's word for automation rules**, and `00-context.md §4` flags that "Launch strategy" *already collides* with two in-code meanings (the `fill_first|equal|duplicate` distribution enum and AutoPilot's profile type). *Evidence-based caution: the IA must disambiguate FabAds' "Launch Strategy" from both the in-code enums and the category's "automation Strategy" usage.*

5. **The numeric/Brazilian labels need an internal canon + a user-education surface.** Because **151/135/115 and Bruno-Brazilian are [not-found] publicly**, two things follow: (a) the FabAds team must define them authoritatively (already the plan), and (b) since users *won't* find a definition by Googling, each predefined strategy in the UI should carry a **plain-language "what this builds + when to use it"** description — recognition-over-recall, and it doubles as the differentiator competitors' bare config-presets lack.

6. **If a numeric strategy implies many ad sets, label it a *testing* flow explicitly.** The consolidation consensus penalizes >5 ad sets *for scaling* (§1 row 1), while high-ad-set "spray" structures are accepted *for testing* (§1 rows 8–9). Mapping each predefined strategy to the right MOM category (testing vs scaling) keeps it defensible against the prevailing best-practice narrative.
